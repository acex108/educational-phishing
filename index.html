Three quick takeaways

  • 1 Pause before clicking.
    Be cautious with unexpected links or attachments.
  • 2 Check the details.
    Review sender addresses, URLs, tone, and requests.
  • 3 Report suspicious messages.
    Use the college's approved reporting process.

Warning signs commonly found in phishing emails

Sender

Unexpected or mismatched sender address

The display name may look familiar, but the actual email address may come from a non-college domain or contain subtle misspellings.

Link

Suspicious or unfamiliar URL

A link may appear legitimate in the email text but lead somewhere else. Hover over links to preview the destination before clicking.

Urgency

Pressure to act immediately

Phishing messages often claim that your account will be locked, your access will expire, or immediate action is required.

Request

Requests for passwords or MFA codes

Be suspicious of any message asking you to provide a password, approve a login, confirm an MFA code, or verify personal information.

Tone

Generic greeting or unusual wording

Messages that use vague greetings, awkward phrasing, or language that does not match normal college communications may be suspicious.

Branding

Formatting or branding inconsistencies

Incorrect logos, low-quality images, unusual colors, broken layouts, or inconsistent signatures can indicate a fraudulent message.

What should you do with suspicious emails?

  1. Do not click additional links or open unexpected attachments.
  2. Do not enter your username, password, MFA code, student ID, employee ID, or other sensitive information.
  3. Report the message using your college's approved reporting method.
  4. If you believe you entered credentials on a real suspicious site, contact the Office of Information Technology immediately.
